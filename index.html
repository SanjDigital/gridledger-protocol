PUBLIC TECHNICAL STATUS
GL-1 is a proposed deterministic methodology demonstrated at one reference node. Internal replay has been demonstrated; external independent replay, broader generalisation, and institutional usefulness remain pending validation.
The paper explains the research question. This page explains what the protocol actually does—and what it does not claim.

01 The Measurement Problem

Many institutions make decisions about productive activity without independently verifiable operational evidence. The GL-1 Protocol addresses this condition—the Measurement Gap—by defining a deterministic methodology for constructing operational evidence records from defined source data.

The Measurement Gap is the difference between productive reality and what institutions can independently verify.

02 What GL-1 Does

The GL-1 Protocol defines how heterogeneous operational records are transformed into sealed evidence records whose processing can be reconstructed under version-locked rules.

  • ✓Defines source records, parsing rules, canonical fields, and verification states.
  • ✓Produces Operational Evidence Records and evidence packages with provenance metadata.
  • ✓Preserves deterministic outputs and explicit failure states for later inspection.
The protocol does not establish creditworthiness, financial viability, future performance, or institutional decisions. It produces evidence that institutions may evaluate within their own governance frameworks.

03 The Operational Evidence Record

An Operational Evidence Record (OER) is a sealed operational record produced from defined evidence sources under version-locked governance and documented replay procedures, for which the verification computation is designed to be independently replayable.

Source

Defined operational, energy, production, and payment records.

Process

GL-1 parsing, normalisation, reconciliation, and sealing.

Output

OER → Evidence Package → institutional consumer.

Raw source records—including ESCOM token records, operator production reports, and payment-platform records—are parsed, normalised, reconciled, sealed, and anchored. Evidence is generated once, transformed deterministically, and rendered many times.

07 Sealing & Provenance

Each OER is sealed with a cryptographic hash over canonicalised inputs. The seal chain is anchored to a public Trust Anchor, creating a tamper-evident record of the verification computation. A seal establishes integrity of the recorded computation; it does not independently prove that an underlying real-world event occurred exactly as represented.

08 Fail-Closed Rules

Where required evidence is absent, the protocol does not infer the missing fact. It preserves the evidentiary state as EVIDENCE_FAILURE rather than manufacturing a reconciliation.

09 Reference Implementation

The current reference implementation processes a single node: Nabiwi, Malawi. The canonical ledger contains 707 sealed cycles. The 2025-04-02 to 2025-08-21 review window contains 63 cycles, of which 62 satisfy the clean-cycle predicate. The clean-row ratio is 98.4% (62/63); aggregate monetary adherence is 97.9% (MWK 3,015,450 actual / MWK 3,080,653 expected).

These are internal canonical-ledger findings, not external validation, credit assessments, or evidence of generalisation.

10 Known Limitations

  • ×One verified reference node.
  • ×No external hostile replay or independent validation has been completed.
  • ×No regulated capital has been deployed against outputs.
  • ×No institutional reliance has been demonstrated.
  • ×Gate 3B remains INCONCLUSIVE — EVIDENCE DEFICIT: 711 staging records could not be causally linked to 6,383 loaded receipt records because no shared provenance key was present.
  • ×Generalisation across nodes and sectors has not been established.

11 P-DFR & Settlement Provenance

Testing of the reference corpus demonstrated a practical limitation: operational and financial records may exist on separate evidentiary planes. Where source-level provenance does not link them, the protocol preserves that uncertainty rather than manufacturing a reconciliation.

P-DFR V1.4—Provenance Capture is a proposed architectural response. It would define a method for embedding a deterministic cycle hash in the remittance reference field at the point of origin. It remains a design proposal requiring implementation and independent validation.

13 AMI-Neutral Architecture

AMI measures the grid. GridLedger reconciles the evidence.

The protocol is designed to operate across the transition from pre-AMI systems to AMI and post-AMI environments. The fundamental problem is reconciliation across evidence systems, not simply the absence of smart meters.
